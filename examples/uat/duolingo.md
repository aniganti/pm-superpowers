## Hypothesized primary moats

Gamified engagement (feedback loops). The learning dataset and experiment flywheel (data). Brand ("synonymous with language learning"). Learner scale. Switching costs are a stated weakness, not a moat: the filing says they are low, and the business still depends on engagement that those low costs can erase.

## Quotes used

**Item 1 — engagement.** Gamification features motivate learners; the company runs thousands of A/B tests. "there were about 43 million daily active users with a 7-day streak or longer."

**Item 1 — data.** "Our millions of learners complete nearly 2 billion exercises every day, creating what we believe to be the world's largest learning dataset." Growth "driven by two mutually-reinforcing flywheels: our learning flywheel and our investment flywheel."

**Item 1 — brand and scale.** "For many, Duolingo has become synonymous with language learning." Competitive factors include "Our ability to maintain the value and reputation of our brand" and "The scale, growth and engagement of our learners relative to those of our competitors." "more than 130 million monthly active users."

**Item 1 — Competition (no companies named).** Other online and app-based language platforms, offline language learning, assessment providers, and literacy platforms.

**Item 1A — the moat risk, in the filing's words.** "The online language learning industry is highly competitive, with low switching costs and a consistent stream of new products and entrants." A new product could gain scale through generative AI. Free alternatives include chatbots. A separate heading covers failure to keep users or a drop in engagement.

**MD&A (context).** About 52.7 million DAUs for the quarter ended 2025-12-31, up 30% from 40.5 million.
