## Hypothesized primary moats

Payment credentials × acceptance footprint (network); global brand; security and reliability; breadth of CMS and value-added services. Regulation is the top risk class and also a barrier. It is not a reason to invent a rating outside None / Emerging / Moderate / Strong.

## Quotes used

**Item 1 — scale.** "we had nearly 5 billion payment credentials, which are issued Visa card accounts, that were available to be used at more than 175 million merchant locations worldwide." Total payments and cash volume was $17 trillion.

**Item 1 — Competition, self-described advantage.** "well-positioned competitively due to our global brand, our broad set of payment products, CMS offerings and value-added services and our proven track record of processing payment transactions securely and reliably."

**Item 1 — named and categorized competitors.** Global networks: "American Express, Diners Club/Discover (now owned by Capital One), JCB, Mastercard and UnionPay." Also local debit networks, closed commerce ecosystems, BNPL, cryptocurrency platforms including stablecoins, real-time payment networks, digital wallets, processors, and CMS / value-added-service providers.

**Item 1 — data / tech.** Visa Advanced Authorization uses machine learning to identify fraud on Visa card payments (Risk and Security Solutions).

**Item 1A — regulation (opens the risk factors).** "We are subject to complex and evolving global regulations that could harm our business and financial results." The next headings include "Increased scrutiny and regulation of the global payments industry, including with respect to interchange reimbursement fees" and litigation or investigations. Later: "We face intense competition in our industry."

**Item 1A — disintermediation.** Parties that access Visa credentials, tokens, and technologies might steer account holders to alternative payment methods, "if we experience brand disintermediation."
