## Hypothesized primary moats

Economies of scale (limited SKUs, volume purchasing, lower gross margin). Membership loyalty (a feedback loop). Brand (Kirkland Signature and pricing authority). Network effects are not a moat this business depends on.

## Quotes used

**Item 1 — the model is scale.** "offering low prices on a limited selection of nationally-branded and private-label products in a wide range of categories will produce high sales volumes and rapid inventory turnover. When combined with the operating efficiencies achieved by volume purchasing…" The filing also states fewer than 4,000 active SKUs per warehouse.

**Item 1 — membership.** "Our member renewal rate was 92.3% in the U.S. and Canada and 89.8% worldwide at the end of 2026."

**Item 1 — brand.** "our well-recognized brands, including the Costco Wholesale trademarks and our private-label brand, Kirkland Signature."

**Item 1 — Competition.** "Walmart, Target, Kroger, and Amazon are among our significant general merchandise retail competitors in the U.S. We also compete with other warehouse clubs, including Walmart's Sam's Club and BJ's Wholesale Club."

**Item 1A — loyalty and brand.** "Our failure to maintain membership growth, loyalty, brand recognition, and our culture could adversely affect our results of operations."

**Item 1A — digital.** "If we do not successfully develop and maintain a relevant and comprehensive digital experience for our members…"

**Item 1A — competition.** Warehouse clubs and other retailers; online and mobile channels make comparison shopping easier.

**MD&A (context).** Net sales increased 10% to $297,247 million. Membership fee revenue increased 11% to $5,907 million. The membership format "is designed to reinforce member loyalty and provide continuing fee revenue."
