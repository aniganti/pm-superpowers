# Strategy Frameworks Reference

Shared framework definitions referenced by skills in this marketplace.

---

## Rumelt's Strategy Kernel

Good strategy answers three questions (from Richard Rumelt, *Good Strategy, Bad Strategy*):

1. **The 'Why'** — Diagnosis of the problem and generating insights
2. **The 'What'** — Guiding policies for actions to be taken
3. **The 'How'** — Actionable objectives to solve the problem

---

## 6-Step Product Strategy Framework

Extending the strategy kernel to digital products:

1. **Understand the market and users** — User research, personas, market segmentation, competitive landscape, regulatory environment
2. **Understand the problem space** — Problem statements (WHO/WHAT/WHY format), macro/micro economic forces, pain point prioritization
3. **Identify key pillars of strategy** — VRIO analysis for competitive advantages, cross-functional stakeholder input
4. **Seek design and technical alignment** — System designs, architecture diagrams, service blueprints, user flows, risk identification
5. **Define vision, mission, and goals** — Product vision (aspirational), mission (objectives), north-star metric, KPIs per initiative
6. **Put it all together and communicate** — Strategy document, FAQ for pushbacks, socialize iteratively, focus on pillars not execution

---

## VRIO Framework

Evaluates resources and capabilities for competitive advantage:

| Question | If No → | If Yes → |
|----------|---------|----------|
| **Valuable?** Does it exploit opportunities or neutralize threats? | Competitive Disadvantage | Continue → |
| **Rare?** Is it controlled by few competing firms? | Competitive Parity | Continue → |
| **Costly to Imitate?** Is it expensive for others to develop? | Temporary Competitive Advantage | Continue → |
| **Organized?** Is the firm organized to capture its value? | Unused Competitive Advantage | **Sustained Competitive Advantage** |

## Fogg Behaviour Model (B = MAP)

From Stanford's Behaviour Design Lab. Explains when a target behaviour occurs:

- **B** = Behaviour (an action a user must do)
- **M** = Motivation of a user to perform the action
- **A** = Ability of the user to perform the action
- **P** = Prompt — the extrinsic or intrinsic trigger to develop the new behaviour

When a desired behaviour does not happen, a combination of motivation, ability, or prompts are missing.

**Extrinsic prompts**: External triggers (notification sounds, trends, calls to action)
**Intrinsic prompts**: Driven by emotions developed through previous experiences (the real power — habitual usage)

## Aggregation Theory

From Ben Thompson: *Aggregation Theory describes how platforms (i.e. aggregators) come to dominate the industries in which they compete in a systematic and predictable way.*

### Value Chain

Three groups in a B2C value chain:
1. **Suppliers** — Produce goods/services
2. **Distributors** — Deliver to market
3. **Consumers** — End users

### Integration Strategies

- **Backward integration** (pre-digital): Control supply + distribution (e.g., NYC taxi medallions + dispatching)
- **Forward integration** (digital): Build exclusive consumer relationships — the internet commoditized supply and made distribution free, so the only thing left to monopolize is consumers

### Product Ecosystem

The most successful companies build a suite of products that sustain each other in a closed-loop ecosystem, capturing the entire value chain. Each product should support and uphold others, contributing to strategic initiatives and growth of the whole.

## Tigers / Paper Tigers / Elephants (Pre-Mortem)

Risk categorization framework for pre-mortem analysis:

- **Tigers** — Real problems based on evidence, past experience, or clear logic. Should keep you up at night. Require action.
- **Paper Tigers** — Problems others might worry about, but are unlikely or overblown. Worth documenting to align stakeholders. Not worth significant resource investment.
- **Elephants** — Unspoken concerns the team is not discussing enough. Could be real; uncertain. Deserve investigation before launch.

### Tiger Urgency Classification

- **Launch-Blocking** — Must be solved before launch (e.g., core feature broken, regulatory blocker)
- **Fast-Follow** — Must be solved within 30 days post-launch (e.g., performance issues, secondary features)
- **Track** — Monitor post-launch; solve if it becomes an issue (e.g., nice-to-have features, edge cases)

## Problem Statement Format

Structure problem statements as:

> "[User segment] needs a way to [job/need] because [root cause of pain]"

Examples:
- "Millennials need a way to easily buy everyday essentials from their mobile device because they struggle to prioritize apparel shopping due to their unpredictable schedules"
- "E-commerce merchants struggle to attract and sell to subprime customers because they lack the ability to offer instant credit and miss out on this customer segment entirely"
